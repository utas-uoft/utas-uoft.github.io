The Agent
Engineering Track

A hands-on cohort that meets once a week. You build an agent, then you build the thing that tells you whether it works. Capacity-limited, and open to every program.

01

The six weeks

  1. 01What an agent isThe loop, the tools, and what the model can see on any given turn.
  2. 02Tool design and MCPWriting tools a model can use without guessing what you meant.
  3. 03Context engineeringDeciding what goes into the window, and what earns its place there.
  4. 04EvaluationThe week most courses skip. How to know whether the thing works at all.
  5. 05Reliability, cost, latencyFailure modes, retries, and what an agent costs to run.
  6. 06Capstone and demo nightYou show what you built to a room of people.
02

What you leave with

Three things, not a certificate.

an agent

Something you built yourself, running, with the tools you designed for it.

an eval suite

The part almost nobody has. It tells you whether your agent works, and it keeps telling you after you change something.

And a demo you can show someone: an employer, a professor, or the next person you're trying to convince to join.

03

Who it's for

You don't need an ML background.

Building good agents is mostly about thinking clearly about systems. What tools it should have, what context it needs, how you would know if it worked. If you have ever mapped out a messy process and found the step where it breaks, you already have the core skill.

The people who understand a domain are the ones who know what an agent in it should do. That is why we want law, commerce, life sci and humanities students in the cohort, and not as a courtesy.

Bring a laptop. That's the prerequisite.
