Research

Agent Lab

Small pods that take real agent systems apart, put them back together, and publish what they found. Six weeks a cycle, one artifact each, in the open.

01

How a pod works

Publish, or the pod dissolves.

Three to five people. Six weeks. One public artifact at the end: a writeup and a repo in the society's GitHub org, under the society's name.

That rule is the whole design. A research group with no deadline turns into a reading group, and a reading group is quietly dead by November. We would rather ship something small on time than plan something ambitious that never lands.

Negative results count. They are often the useful ones.

02

Year one

Three kinds of cycle.

  1. 01 ReplicationTake a published agentic result and reproduce it. Report what broke. This is the best first project anyone can do.
  2. 02 TeardownReverse-engineer a deployed agent product from its public documentation and behaviour. Architecture, prompting, and how it is evaluated.
  3. 03 Eval buildConstruct a small task suite for one domain, then measure two or three agent frameworks against it.

After two completed cycles with artifacts to show, one pod moves on to original work aimed at a real venue: a new benchmark, or an open-source release that other people actually use. That is earned by track record rather than by the calendar.

03

Joining a pod

The workshops feed the Lab.

Most pod members come out of the workshops, because by then you have already built an agent and written evals for it. That is the whole prerequisite.

Pods are capacity-limited, but membership in the society is not. Anyone registered at U of T can join, come to the lectures, and put their name down for the next cycle.
