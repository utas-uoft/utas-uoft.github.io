01

The gap

No course at U of T teaches agent evaluation.

AI agents went from demo to deployed in about eighteen months. Employers now hire for the skills that make them work: designing tools, managing context, evaluating outputs, handling failure, controlling cost and latency.

Context engineering isn't taught either, and no student group on campus covers this ground.

So we're building one.

02

The distinction

Systems, not models.

Most AI work is

model-centric

You train a model, read an architecture paper, push a benchmark. It's research, and you need a machine learning background to take part.

Agentic work is

systems-centric

Orchestration, tool interfaces, context, evaluation, failure modes, cost and latency. The work looks more like reliability engineering than machine learning.

That's why you can start here without a machine learning background and still go deep. We want law, commerce, life sci and humanities students in the room, because the work is worse without them.

03

What we do

Two halves, wired together.

Research

Agent Lab

Pods of three to five people work in six-week cycles. Each pod publishes one artifact per cycle under the society's name: a writeup and a repo.

Year one runs replication, then teardown, then eval-building. After two completed cycles, one pod moves to original work aimed at a real venue.

Publish, or the pod dissolves. We would rather ship something small on time than plan something ambitious that never lands.

Teaching

Agents in Practice

An open lecture series, free and drop-in, organised by sector: agents in law, in drug discovery, in finance operations, in public-service delivery.

Plus the Agent Engineering Track: six weeks, hands-on, capacity-limited, with a demo night at the end.

No machine learning background required.

Why both

  1. Research pods ship artifacts
  2. We teach from those artifacts
  3. We recruit the next pods at demo night

Clubs that try to do both usually let teaching absorb all the labour, and the research side goes quiet by November. Making each half depend on the other is how we plan to avoid that.
